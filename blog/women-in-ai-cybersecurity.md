## The State of the Field: Where Women Stand

The numbers are improving — slowly.

Women represent approximately 24% of the cybersecurity workforce globally (ISC² 2023). In AI research and development, the number drops to around 22%. At the intersection — AI security — the numbers are even thinner.

But the pipeline is growing. More women are entering STEM earlier, more are pursuing cybersecurity certifications, and more are building AI tools that are reshaping how security operations work.

The question isn't whether women will be in AI cybersecurity. We already are. The question is whether we'll be in the rooms where the most consequential decisions get made.

## Why Women Bring Something Specific to AI Safety

This isn't about soft skills or emotional intelligence tropes. This is about operational diversity.

Research consistently shows that diverse teams identify more attack vectors, ask different questions about system design, and catch failure modes that homogeneous teams miss.

In AI security specifically, that matters because:

**1. Prompt injection attacks require creative adversarial thinking.** Different cognitive approaches catch different edge cases. A team that all thinks the same way will miss the same things.

**2. AI systems reflect the assumptions of their builders.** If everyone building AI security tools has the same background, those tools will have the same blind spots. Women in these roles — especially those from non-traditional paths — are disruption insurance.

**3. Threat modeling benefits from life experience.** Social engineering, phishing, and human-centered attacks require understanding how different types of people behave. Diverse teams are simply better at this.
